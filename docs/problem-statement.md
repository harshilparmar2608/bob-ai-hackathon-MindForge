# Problem Statement

## Background

The problem belongs to the **Education Technology (EdTech) and Career Development** domain, particularly student success, academic management, and placement readiness. In modern colleges, students manage information across disconnected platforms such as learning management systems, messaging apps, email, calendars, coding platforms, and placement portals, creating information overload and making it difficult to prioritize academics and career goals effectively.


## The Problem

Students manage academic and career information across **5–7 disconnected platforms**—such as learning-management systems, WhatsApp, email, calendars, coding platforms, and placement portals—making it difficult to identify their most urgent tasks. As a result, students frequently **miss deadlines, under-prepare for exams, and overlook internships or placement opportunities** because there is no single system that continuously analyzes their workload, performance, and career goals and tells them what to prioritize next.

## Who is Affected

**Primary users are undergraduate and postgraduate college students**, especially those balancing coursework, multiple assignments, exams, coding practice, internships, hackathons, and placement preparation simultaneously. The problem is most acute for **students in their 2nd–4th years who manage academic work alongside career preparation and rely on multiple disconnected platforms to track deadlines, study materials, and opportunities**.

## Why It Matters

The problem costs students **significant time and missed opportunities** because they must manually check multiple platforms and decide what to prioritize. Missed assignments, inefficient exam preparation, and overlooked internships or placement opportunities can negatively affect **academic performance, employability, and career outcomes**, while also creating substantial stress and information overload.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Students currently use a combination of **Google Classroom/LMS, WhatsApp, email, calendars, to-do apps, coding platforms, and placement portals** to track their academic and career activities. These tools work independently and mainly provide information or reminders; they **do not understand the student’s overall workload, identify what should be prioritized, predict academic risks, or connect academic performance with career goals**, leaving students to manually organize everything themselves.
